# Emotion Engine

Governs human performance: facial expression, breathing, tears, silence, and
emotional intensity. This sits alongside `voice-bible.md` and
`personality-bible.md` — a character's emotional performance in any given
moment must be consistent with their established personality and voice, not a
generic performance layered on top of them.

## Global rule

Characters feel before they speak. Every line of dialogue should carry
believable emotion through more than just the words — through:

- Eyes
- Facial muscles
- Breathing
- Body posture
- Voice delivery
- Natural pauses
- Hand movement

Never produce a robotic or emotionally flat performance. A line delivered with
no physical or vocal emotional signal reads as artificial, even if the words
themselves are right.

## Emotion scale

Use this as a reference point for describing performance intensity in a prompt,
not a rigid 0-6 counter to track mechanically — the point is calibrating how
much physical/vocal signal a moment needs.

**Level 0 — Emotionless**
Neutral face, steady breathing, minimal movement

**Level 1 — Calm**
Soft eye contact, relaxed shoulders, gentle voice

**Level 2 — Concern**
Slight frown, slower speech, small pauses

**Level 3 — Fear**
Faster breathing, trembling hands, wide eyes, unsteady voice

**Level 4 — Anger**
Tight jaw, raised voice, aggressive posture, heavy breathing

**Level 5 — Grief**
Tears, voice breaking, long silence, shaking body, difficulty speaking

**Level 6 — Joy**
Genuine smile, bright eyes, natural laughter, energetic movement

## Performance rules for specific emotions

**Crying**
Never cut straight to crying. Let it progress naturally: wet eyes → trembling
lips → tears → broken voice. A performance that jumps straight to full tears
reads as unearned.

**Fear**
Show before telling — let breathing, eye movement, and hesitation carry the
fear rather than jumping straight to shouting or screaming.

**Anger**
Build intensity gradually rather than starting at full volume. Avoid constant
screaming; anger reads as more threatening when it's controlled and rises, not
when it's maxed out from the first line.

**Love**
Use subtle expression, comfortable silence, and softer vocal delivery rather
than overstated gestures.

## Silence as dialogue

Silence is itself a performance choice, not empty space to fill. Allow
emotional pauses after shocking revelations, deaths, betrayals, or heartfelt
moments — don't rush a conversation past a beat that needs room to land.

## Group/crowd emotion

Crowds and background characters should react in a way appropriate to the
setting, not as generic filler motion:

- Church → worship, tears, applause
- Hospital → concern, whispering
- Football stadium → explosive celebration
- Funeral → restrained grief
- Courtroom → tense silence

## Continuity lock

A character cannot jump from grief to happiness (or between any two
emotional extremes) within the same scene unless the screenplay provides a
believable transition between them. Maintain emotional continuity across
connected shots the same way face, voice, and personality are maintained —
an unexplained emotional jump is a continuity error, not a performance choice.

## How this relates to personality and voice

A character's emotional baseline and how they express a given emotion level
should already be defined in their entry in `character-bible.md` (see
`personality-bible.md`'s "Emotional trigger" and "Stress behavior" fields, and
`voice-bible.md`'s tone/pitch fields). This module governs the moment-to-moment
performance; the bible governs what that performance should characteristically
look like for this specific character. For example: two characters both at
"Level 4 — Anger" should still look different from each other if their
established stress behavior differs (one goes quiet and controlled, another
raises their voice) — apply the emotion scale through the lens of the
character's own established behavior, not as a one-size-fits-all performance.

## Where this fits in the self-review pass

Add to Step 4 of SKILL.md: does the emotional performance in this clip match
the intensity the scene calls for, does it follow natural progression rather
than jumping straight to a peak (especially for crying/fear/anger), and is any
emotional shift from the previous connected shot justified by what's shown on
screen?
