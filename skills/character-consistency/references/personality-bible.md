# Character Personality Bible

A character's personality is permanent — it cannot change except through visible,
on-screen character development. Appearance and voice identify a character;
personality is what makes an audience believe the character is real, so drift here
matters as much as a face or voice changing.

## Global rule

Maintain the same behavior, emotional reactions, intelligence, values, sense of
humor, confidence level, fears, and decision-making style for a character across
every scene/episode of a project, unless a story beat has already shown a reason
for that character to change.

## Behavior check before writing dialogue or action for a character

Before finalizing a line of dialogue or an action beat for an established
character, check:

- Would this character actually say this?
- Would they react this way, given their established temperament and emotional
  triggers?
- Is this decision consistent with their motivation and moral compass as already
  established?

If the answer to any of these is no, rewrite the dialogue or action rather than
letting it stand — this is part of the Step 4 self-review pass in SKILL.md, not a
separate step to skip.

## Development rule

A character's personality may evolve, but only through events actually shown on
screen — a believable story arc (a betrayal, a loss, a hard-won victory) that
justifies the shift. Never let a character's personality drift between scenes
without that visible cause; an unexplained shift is inconsistency, not
development. When development is intentional, log it the same way an aging or
wardrobe change is logged (see `continuity-log-template.md`), noting which scene
the shift occurs at and what on-screen event justifies it.
