# Camera Angle Bible

Defines the camera's position, height, and viewing angle for a given shot. Use
alongside `shot-grammar.md` (movement/lighting/composition) and the Shot
Hierarchy when planning a scene.

## Primary camera angles

**Angle 1 — Eye level**
Default storytelling angle. Natural conversations and most dialogue scenes.

**Angle 2 — Low angle**
Camera below the subject, looking up. Makes a character feel powerful,
dangerous, or dominant.

**Angle 3 — High angle**
Camera above, looking down. Creates a sense of vulnerability, loneliness, or
weakness in the subject.

**Angle 4 — Bird's-eye view**
90° top-down. Best for cities, battlefields, crowds, and establishing geography
at a glance.

**Angle 5 — Drone view**
Moving aerial shot. Use only outdoors, for reveals, travel, or large
environments — an indoor "drone-style" move without justification reads as
gimmicky (see Forbidden list in `shot-grammar.md`).

**Angle 6 — Over-the-shoulder**
Standard dialogue framing. Keep the 180° rule (consistent screen direction)
intact across cuts within the conversation.

**Angle 7 — POV**
Shows exactly what a character sees. Use sparingly — it's an immersive, high-
impact tool that loses power if overused.

**Angle 8 — Insert / macro**
Extreme close-up on an important object: keys, a phone, eyes, a weapon, a
document. Pairs with the "Insert shot" step in the Shot Hierarchy.

## Camera height system

Choose height based on what the moment needs, not habit:

| Height | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Ground | Footsteps, action beats, vehicles |
| Knee | Walking sequences |
| Waist | Medium dialogue |
| Chest | Emotional conversations |
| Eye (default) | Natural human perspective |
| Overhead | Tactical or architectural view |
| Sky | Drone and landscape reveals |

## Applying this alongside continuity rules

Within one continuous scene, don't change camera height or angle without a
reason tied to the story (a power shift, a reveal, a character being knocked
down). See the Continuity section of `shot-grammar.md` for the full rule on
holding height, screen direction, lighting direction, and lens choice steady
across a scene unless a perspective change is intentional.
