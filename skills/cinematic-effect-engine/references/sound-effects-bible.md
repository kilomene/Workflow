# Sound Effects Bible

Sound effects are part of world continuity, on the same footing as the visual
continuity rules elsewhere in this skill. Every object, movement, and
environment visible in a shot must produce realistic, matching audio.

## Global rule

Generate synchronized, high-quality cinematic Foley and environmental sound for
what's on screen.

- Never leave an important action silent — a door closing, a footstep, a gunshot,
  an impact all need audio, not just the visual
- Never use a random or mismatched sound effect that doesn't correspond to what's
  actually happening in the scene

## Environmental SFX by category

**Wind**
Gentle breeze, strong wind, storm gusts, leaves rustling, whistling through
buildings

**Rain**
Light drizzle, heavy rainfall, thunder, water splashes, roof impacts

**Fire**
Crackling flames, burning wood, explosions, ember ambience, heat roar

**Water**
Ocean waves, river current, waterfalls, footsteps in puddles, underwater
ambience

**City**
Traffic, distant sirens, car horns, crowd chatter, construction

**Nature**
Birds, insects, frogs, jungle ambience, night crickets

## Action SFX by category

**Gunshot**
Caliber-specific gunfire, muzzle blast, bullet whizz, shell casing drop, echo
matched to the location's acoustics

**Fight**
Punch impacts, body hits, cloth movement, grunts, foot shuffles

**Vehicles**
Engine idle, acceleration, tire screech, brake hiss, door slam

## Object Foley

Keys jingling, phone vibration, paper movement, glass breaking, chair scraping,
door creak, metal impacts — any handled or interacted-with object needs its own
matching sound, not silence or a generic substitute.

## Location-based SFX library

Beyond the general environmental/action/object categories above, select sound
by the specific type of location the scene is set in — each location has its own
natural sound palette:

**Hospital**
Ambulance siren (near or distant), ECG heart monitor, oxygen machine, wheelchair
movement, hospital footsteps, PA announcement, automatic sliding doors

**Emergency**
Fire alarm, smoke detector beeps, emergency evacuation speakers, police siren,
rescue radio chatter

**Home & office**
Clock ticking, wall clock chime, keyboard typing, computer notification, printer,
air conditioner, elevator ding

**Phone**
Incoming ringtone, vibration, notification ping, message received, call
disconnect, voice message alert

**Stadium**
Massive crowd cheering, chants, referee whistle, ball kick impact, goal
celebration, stadium announcer, vuvuzela (only where culturally/contextually
appropriate to the specific match/setting)

**Street football**
Children cheering, concrete ball bounce, laughter, street ambience, bicycle
passing, neighborhood voices

**Motor racing**
Engine revving, turbo spool, gear shifts, tire screech, crowd roar, pit crew
tools

**Church**
Choir singing, congregational worship, organ, microphone reverb, applause,
wooden pew movement, church bell

**City**
Traffic, horns, bus stopping, market voices, distant construction, wind through
buildings

**Weather (indoor-heard)**
Thunder, heavy rain, drizzle, wind gust, tree movement, window rattling — the
sound of weather as heard from inside a structure, distinct from the outdoor
weather SFX above

### Golden rule: never mix unrelated environments

Use only the sounds that naturally exist in the scene's current location. Don't
carry over ambience from a different location type. For example: hospital sounds
must never appear inside a football stadium, unless the story intentionally and
visibly transitions between the two locations.

## Spatial audio rule

Sound must match distance and space, not play at one flat volume regardless of
what the camera shows:

- **Near** = loud and detailed
- **Far** = quieter, with natural echo/delay appropriate to the distance
- **Behind walls / obstructed** = muffled
- **Open spaces** = wider, more natural reverb

## Continuity lock

If a sound-producing element is visible, its sound must be present — this is the
audio equivalent of the visual continuity locks elsewhere in this skill:

- If rain is visible, rain ambience must be audible
- If fire is visible, the sound of flames must be present
- If a gun fires, include muzzle blast, shell casing drop, and an environmental
  echo matched to the space
- Never let visuals and sound separate — a visibly stormy scene with no wind or
  rain audio, or a visible gunfight with no gunfire audio, is a continuity error,
  not a stylistic choice

## Professional layering

A premium, Netflix-style scene typically runs four audio layers simultaneously,
each doing a distinct job:

| Layer | Example |
|---|---|
| Dialogue | Character voices |
| Foley | Footsteps, clothes, doors |
| Environment | Wind, rain, birds, city |
| Music | Choir, orchestra, suspense score |

See `music-bible.md` for the music layer specifically — including the rule that
music sits underneath the other three layers rather than competing with dialogue
or overwhelming Foley/environment detail.

## Where this fits in the self-review pass

Add to the Quality self-check in SKILL.md: does every visible sound-producing
element (weather, fire, impacts, handled objects) have matching audio, is it
scaled correctly for on-screen distance, and — if this platform generates audio
at all — are the four layers (dialogue, Foley, environment, music) present and
balanced rather than collapsed into one flat track?
