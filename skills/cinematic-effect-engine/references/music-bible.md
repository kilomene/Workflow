# Background Music Bible

Music supports emotion — it must never overpower dialogue. Choose music based on
the scene's setting, culture, and emotional register, not as a generic
"cinematic score" default.

This is one of four audio layers (dialogue, Foley, environment, music) that run
together in a premium scene — see `sound-effects-bible.md` for the other three
and the full layering table.

## Global rule

Match music to what the scene actually is — its setting, culture, and emotional
beat — rather than applying one all-purpose orchestral sound to every scene.

## Scene-based music palette

**Church**
- Choir
- Chorus
- Gospel orchestra
- Organ
- Soft worship vocals

**Royal / kingdom**
- Orchestral score drawing on the setting's actual culture (e.g. African
  orchestral score for an African kingdom setting — match the specific culture
  depicted, not a generic "regal" sound)
- Brass
- Deep percussion
- Strings

**Action**
- Hybrid cinematic orchestra
- Taiko or similarly percussive drums
- Pulsing tension rhythms

**Romance**
- Piano
- Soft strings
- Ambient textures

**Suspense**
- Low drones
- Minimal cello
- Heartbeat-like percussion

**Tragedy**
- Emotional orchestra
- Solo violin
- Choir pads

**Victory**
- Full orchestra
- Choir
- Inspirational brass

## Silence rule

If dialogue is already carrying the emotional weight of a scene, reduce the
music or remove it entirely. Silence is an intentional cinematic tool, not an
absence to fill — a quiet, unscored moment can be more powerful than underscoring
it.

## Cultural authenticity

Music should match the world's actual culture, language, religion, and location
as established in the story. Don't insert a genre or instrumentation that clashes
with the seriousness or cultural setting of a scene (e.g. an incongruous genre
choice under a solemn or sacred moment). When a scene is set in a specific real-
world culture, prefer instrumentation and style genuinely associated with that
culture over a generic substitute.

## Where this fits in the self-review pass

Add to the Quality self-check in SKILL.md: does the music choice match the
scene's setting/culture/emotion, and — if dialogue is present — is the music
sitting underneath it rather than competing with it?
