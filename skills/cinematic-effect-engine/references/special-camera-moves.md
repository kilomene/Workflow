# Special Camera Moves

High-impact, non-default techniques. These are reserved moves — use sparingly and
only when the moment specifically calls for them, not as a stylistic default.
Pairs with `shot-grammar.md` (standard movement vocabulary) and
`camera-angle-bible.md` (standard angles/heights).

## Extreme telephoto reveal

Use only for high-impact moments: introductions, surveillance, mystery, or
dramatic reveals.

**Shot description:** Begin with an extremely distant view (roughly 500m-3000m
from the subject). The camera behaves like a professional 600-1200mm cinema
telephoto lens, slowly compressing distance while maintaining realistic
perspective until the main character fills the frame.

**Camera specs to describe in the prompt:**
- Lens: 600mm-1200mm telephoto
- Distance: 500m-3000m
- 24fps cinematic motion
- Ultra-stable tripod or gyro stabilization (the move should read as smooth and
  controlled, not handheld)
- Atmospheric haze and realistic heat distortion appropriate to the distance
- Natural depth compression (telephoto compression, not a digital zoom)
- No digital/AI zoom artifacts — this should read as an optical lens move, not a
  crop-and-scale effect

**Movement sequence:**
1. Start with the character almost invisible at extreme distance
2. Slowly close the distance toward the subject
3. Maintain perfect focus tracking throughout — the subject should never fall
   out of focus during the move
4. End on a medium close-up or close-up
5. Keep the movement smooth, deliberate, and suspenseful — this is a slow build,
   not a fast punch-in

**Best uses:**
- Hero introduction
- Secret surveillance
- An assassin or hunter observing a target
- Military reconnaissance
- Stadium or city-scale reveal
- Epic opening scene

**Restriction:** Never use this move repeatedly within a project. It's a
maximum-impact technique that loses its power the moment it becomes a pattern —
reserve it for the one or two moments that genuinely deserve it.
