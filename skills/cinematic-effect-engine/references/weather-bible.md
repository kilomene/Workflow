# Weather Bible

Weather is part of scene continuity, on the same footing as the within-scene
continuity rules in `shot-grammar.md` (camera height, screen direction, lighting
direction). It cannot change mid-scene unless the screenplay/script explicitly
writes a transition.

## Global rule

Across every shot within a continuous scene (and across cuts that are meant to
be the same continuous time period), the environment must preserve:

- Weather condition
- Time of day
- Season
- Cloud density
- Wind intensity
- Rain level
- Fog level
- Ground wetness
- Sun position

## Weather types

- Clear sunny
- Cloudy
- Overcast
- Golden hour
- Blue hour
- Rain
- Storm
- Heavy fog
- Harmattan
- Snow (only if the story's location plausibly allows it)

## Continuity lock

If a scene begins in a specific weather state — say, heavy rain — every
connected shot in that scene must retain the consistent knock-on details, not
just the label "rain":
- Wet clothing on characters already exposed to it
- Wet roads/surfaces
- Consistent rain intensity (don't drift from heavy to light without cause)
- Consistent cloud cover
- Reflections consistent with wet surfaces
- Ambient rain sound, if audio is part of the generation

Don't switch to sunshine, clear skies, or dry surfaces partway through a scene
unless the script includes an explicit weather transition.

## Transition rule

Weather may only change after a written time/scene transition, such as:
- "Two hours later"
- "The following morning"
- "The storm finally stopped"
- "Sunset became night"

Never invent a weather change without one of these (or an equivalent explicit
transition) in the script. An unscripted shift from rain to sun between two
shots that are supposed to be continuous is a continuity error, not a creative
choice — treat it the same way an unscripted change in a character's wardrobe or
face would be treated under `character-consistency`.

## Where this fits in the self-review pass

When running the Quality self-check in SKILL.md, check weather/time-of-day/season
consistency the same way lighting-direction and screen-direction continuity are
checked — it's part of "does this shot match the established continuity of this
scene," not a separate pass.
